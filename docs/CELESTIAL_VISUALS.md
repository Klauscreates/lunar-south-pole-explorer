# Celestial visuals and mission narratives

## 4K visual material upgrade

The active Earth day map, cloud map and Sun map are now **4096 × 2048** JPEG textures from Solar System Scope / INOVE, licensed CC BY 4.0. Provider, source URLs, input/output SHA-256 and measured dimensions are recorded in `apps/web/public/celestial/texture-manifest.json`. Attribution and license appear in Cesium's Data attribution panel. Earth maps were reduced from 8192 × 4096; the provider's `8k_sun.jpg` actually measures 4096 × 2048 and was not upscaled. These are NASA-derived, artist-adjusted display maps, not calibrated data or images of the selected epoch. The prior NASA Blue Marble asset remains in the project but is no longer the active Earth material.

Orbital Earth combines the day/cloud maps, decorative ocean highlights and a thin atmospheric rim with Sun-directed day/night shading. Clouds remain static in the body frame; no weather is predicted. The Sun has an emissive textured surface, display-only limb darkening and a restrained halo. Both use 128-partition surface meshes; glow shells are decorative, not physical occultation radii. These effects do not modify SPICE positions, base-body sizes, Earth attitude, energy or communication gauges. The surface sky view uses the new 4K day/Sun maps but does not add the orbital cloud/halo shader stack. Solar texture orientation is illustrative, not a reconstruction of active regions.

## Orbital explorer

Open **Orbital view** from the main globe. The streamlined interface shows only a compact floating glass date scrubber with playback, a separate close button, and a subtle scale disclosure. It opens centered on the Moon; users can zoom out and explore or double-click empty space for the system overview. Mode/focus selectors and numerical distance panels are no longer displayed. The renderer retains true-scale support internally. Overview uses the same lunar context image on a sphere, not terrain-derived landing detail.

Run `.venv/bin/python -m scripts.export_orbital_scene` to reproduce the 1,009 Oct 1–8, 2026 epochs. Exported Earth/Sun centers use `spkpos(target, et, MOON_ME_DE440_ME421, NONE, MOON)`. These are simultaneous geometric positions in a common rotating, Moon-fixed frame, not observer-apparent sky positions or a full lunar orbit. UTC is converted to ET through the pinned leap-second kernel. DE440 lunar libration comes from the locked binary PCK/frame set. The build and client verify the export SHA-256; invalid frame, timing or rotation matrices fail closed. Missing local kernels fail instead of generating substitute positions.

Earth attitude is `pxform(IAU_EARTH, MOON_ME_DE440_ME421, et)`. This is a time-dependent official text-PCK IAU model, **not precision ITRF/EOP or flight-certified attitude**. Cesium ellipsoid UVs place the texture prime meridian at mesh -X; a 180-degree mesh Z correction maps the equirectangular texture onto the body frame. Historical imagery does not supply live clouds. Neither texture resolution nor Earth attitude changes any existing mission gauge.

True scale preserves exported km-to-meter centers, PCK Earth radii, Sun radius and the existing 1,737,400 m lunar reference globe. Overview preserves center directions and Earth attitude but compresses Earth and Sun separation to 12 and 30 lunar reference radii and sets Moon/Earth/Sun display radii to 2/3/5 lunar reference radii. Both distortions are labeled onscreen. Small visual ambient floors make dark silhouettes legible; shading is decorative, not a radiometric simulation. The Sun uses the emissive display map described above. Shadows/eclipses between celestial bodies are not computed by these overview shaders.

The free camera supports dragging and zooming. Double-click a body for a close-up; double-click empty space to return to the system overview. Touch double-tap uses the same Cesium gesture handler but requires real-device acceptance testing. Timeline playback retains camera offset. The full-screen viewport extends behind the compact translucent scrubber on desktop and phones. Single-body focus hides other bodies' labels to prevent misleading text over the foreground globe. No comparison time-series, terrain visibility or R1 thermal calculations are overwritten.

## Surface sky view (separate apparent-position mode)

Sun/Earth centers are reconstructed from each selected observer's exported apparent Horizons azimuth, elevation and range in the lunar scene's ENU frame. Sphere radii preserve exported angular radii. This is an apparent sky visualization, not a new orbital solver. Terrain depth occludes bodies normally; no always-visible labels or nearby proxy rays remain. The reusable directional light follows the Sun; the existing illumination toggle governs lunar surface lighting.

Surface sky mode uses the 4K Earth day map and Sun display map described above. Earth orientation here is deliberately fixed/illustrative: the operational site export does not contain Earth attitude matrices. Exact timeline-driven continental rotation is available only in the separate orbital mode. Neither mode reconstructs live weather or the solar atmosphere. Legacy inactive Earth asset provenance: NASA/Goddard SVS Blue Marble, https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/ ; 2048 × 1024, SHA-256 ae6214b078ed0864c96f74bcb10ae3021f6eb116f8059797efe0fa9ea8b89d35.

Sky view points toward the selected site's Earth direction from 30 m above its observer position; it is not the lander antenna viewpoint. A submerged/occluded Earth must remain hidden. Backend horizon masks, not the rendered terrain, determine operational geometric indicators.

Mission narratives evaluate both verified site bundles at the selected epoch and shared R1 attitude. They describe assumed-model generation and geometric Earth-center visibility. They never infer battery discharge, heaters, drilling permission or operational RF links. Partial-disk and XTJ coefficient-range warnings override green styling. Missing or non-finite inputs remain unknown. Comparison site selection does not relocate the main globe's selected observer.
